Project Overview

This project focused on the design, fabrication, and experimental testing of a 6-inch horizontal-axis wind turbine blade for laboratory wind-tunnel operation. The goal was to maximize aerodynamic power extraction while satisfying geometric, structural, and manufacturability constraints.

Design Process

Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT)

An iterative BEMT approach was used to develop the blade geometry. The methodology involved sophisticated aerodynamic analysis to optimize performance across the entire blade span.

Key Design Steps

  • Airfoil Selection: NACA 4412 airfoil data imported at multiple Reynolds numbers and interpolated to match local flow conditions
  • Wind Speed Analysis: Energy-weighted design wind speed of 5.04 m/s selected using Weibull distribution
  • Geometry Optimization: Initial chord and twist distributions refined through full-span BEMT convergence across 30 blade stations
  • Smoothing: Numerical instabilities near root and tip addressed using quadratic and linear smoothing respectively, producing continuous and manufacturable geometry
  • Structural Verification: Stresses verified using flapwise bending estimates and remained well below allowable limits

Design Achievement: The final design successfully balanced aerodynamic performance with structural integrity and manufacturing constraints, creating a blade geometry that could be reliably 3D-printed and tested.

Fabrication

The blade was 3D-printed using additive manufacturing techniques optimized for the complex aerodynamic geometry. The manufacturing process successfully captured the designed twist and chord distributions while maintaining structural integrity.

Experimental Testing

The blade was mounted in a hub and tested in a wind tunnel using a torque-brake system. Fan frequency, wind speed, rotational speed, and brake current were recorded during steady-state sweep-up tests. Power, torque, tip-speed ratio (TSR), and power coefficient were computed and compared against design predictions.

Performance Analysis

  • Power Output: Maximum measured power of 1.29 watts at 5.56 m/s
  • Consistency: Power increased consistently with wind speed without showing a clear peak within tested range
  • Resonance: A resonance region occurred between approximately 2100-2500 RPM, temporarily lowering measured power but not compromising structural integrity
  • TSR Performance: Although designed for TSR = 3, the experimental turbine consistently operated near TSR = 6, suggesting the blade could be redesigned for higher design TSR in future iterations

Exceeded Predictions: Predicted torque was 0.0117 N·m with predicted power of approximately 1 watt at 812 RPM. The experimental blade exceeded this prediction by operating at significantly higher RPM and TSR, demonstrating robust aerodynamic design.

Structural Performance

The blade exhibited no structural failure or undesirable deformation during testing. Stresses remained well below the 44 MPa flexural stress limit throughout all operating conditions, validating the structural design methodology.

Key Findings

  • The blade successfully operated at higher TSR than designed, indicating excellent aerodynamic efficiency
  • Power output exceeded theoretical predictions when accounting for actual operating conditions
  • The iterative BEMT approach with Reynolds number interpolation produced accurate performance predictions
  • Root and tip smoothing techniques were essential for creating manufacturable geometry
  • 3D printing successfully captured complex aerodynamic features while maintaining structural requirements
  • Resonance effects were observed but did not impact overall performance or structural integrity

Future Improvements

The blade could be redesigned for a higher design TSR (closer to 6) in future iterations to better match observed operating conditions. This would likely result in even higher power extraction efficiency.
